What to expect

Your first visit, start to finish.

A homoeopathic consultation is mostly a conversation. Here is how ours usually goes.

  1. Tell us everything

    Symptoms, sleep, appetite, what's worrying you. We ask a lot of questions because the odd detail often turns out to be the important one.

  2. A remedy chosen for you

    The prescription is matched to you as a person, not only to the illness on paper. Bach flower remedies can go alongside it when they help.

  3. We review, then adjust

    Follow-up visits track how things are moving and shift the plan as you improve. Treatment should change as you change.

Bring with you

Older prescriptions, recent test reports, and the list of whatever you're taking right now. They save us both a visit's worth of guessing.

Meet your doctor

Twenty years across the table from Kondhwa.

Dr. Farida Dahodwala studied homoeopathic medicine at the Maharashtra University of Health Sciences, Nashik, graduating in 2006, and registered with the state council in 2008. She has spent two decades since, treating the fever that won't break and the eczema that won't settle with the same patience.

Her practice runs on classical homoeopathy, with Bach flower therapy, cosmetology and honey therapy available for people who want gentler paths. She sees children, adults and parents, and she treats sudden complaints and old, stubborn ones side by side.

Questions people actually ask.

Ask the clinic
How do I book an appointment?

Just call +91 90287 69373 and we'll find a time that suits. The clinic runs on appointments so consultations don't get cut short.

How long is the first consultation?

Longer than you're used to, because the details are what the remedy depends on. Plan for an unhurried hour of talking.

Can I continue my current medicines?

Please don't stop anything a doctor has prescribed on your own. Tell us everything you take and we'll advise how homoeopathy sits alongside it.

Is homoeopathy all right for children?

Yes. The clinic sees patients of every age, from infants to grandparents, with doses and advice fitted to each child.

How long will treatment take?

A fever is not eczema. Sudden complaints often settle quickly; long-standing ones take steady follow-ups. We'll be straight with you about which is which.

Is this a replacement for urgent care?

No, and it doesn't claim to be. Anything serious or sudden needs a hospital. In an emergency call 112 or go to the nearest casualty.
